# GOAL-SWITCH-CURRICULUM-001 — real on-policy retarget training, negative result

**17 November 2026 · techrote/DRIV3R · consolidated Draft/HOLD PR #1 · synthetic DEVELOPMENT only.**

## Question and source-matched protocol

Does supervised **on-policy training with deliberate mid-trajectory target changes** improve 128–160-decision performance compared with a matched on-policy policy that never switches its TRAIN target? This is an **independent protocol variant of the already published RETARGET-DAGGER-001**, itself following GOAL-RETARGET-001: it uses fresh DEVELOPMENT seeds (j=2 rather than j=1), no evaluation-time forced moves, a different student-visited curriculum, and longer adaptation (108 rather than 60 extra updates). It is not the first on-policy switch-trained experiment. The new treatment is trained on goal-switch states generated by its **own** actions. Teacher labels are supplied for the current public target, but the teacher does not control subsequent state. This is imitation on student-visited states, **not reinforcement learning** or proven long-horizon planning.

A common tiny recovery-aware 961-parameter prior was trained on TRAIN-only worlds for 20 real Adam updates at each 36/72/128 stage. Two copied adapters then each received 36 real Adam updates at each stage (108 additional optimizer updates/arm). `sham` kept a fixed target during TRAIN; `switch` changed targets at 25%, 52%, 78% of the training horizon. Student actions controlled every TRAIN transition and `STOP` remained in each legal menu. Both saw equal world counts, identical starting prior and equal optimization budgets. Their resulting student-state datasets differed intentionally; seed 620 had unequal sample counts because one arm stopped earlier on some paths, a limitation to training-data matching.

Two independent optimizer seeds, **619 and 620**, used TRAIN synthetic families pickets/ridges/islands/zigzag and optional dead-end corridor challenge. DEVELOPMENT used **new seeds 3002/3102/3202/3302** from those same structural families (not sealed family heldout), normal/trap corridor, and horizons 128 and 160. Every evaluation arm received a predeclared identical sequence of four different targets, switched at decisions **32, 72 and 112**. No model-specific target selection or forced teacher rescue was allowed. `frozen` is the trained common prior and received no extra adaptation.

## Actual results — 96 source-replayed episodes

| Metric | Frozen prior | Sham fixed-target-trained | Switch-target-trained |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| DEVELOPMENT episodes | 32 | 32 | 32 |
| Targets freshly painted in active instruction epoch | 74 | **75** | 71 |
| Mean new painted cells | 138.750 | **142.906** | 141.344 |
| Premature STOP | 4 | **0** | 2 |
| Autonomous host-legal decisions | 4,543 | **4,608** | 4,566 |
| Observed full eight-step switch windows | 94/96 | **96/96** | 96/96 |
| Mean eight-step post-switch Manhattan gain, observed full windows | **6.574** | 6.458 | 6.125 |
| Mean neural probability latency, local CPU | 0.02220 ms | 0.02247 ms | 0.02167 ms |

All arms have the exact same **961 neural parameters** and observe identical legal-action menus; the real optimizer updated two copied policies and all six model files were checkpointed and restored. Evaluation actions are model-selected argmax from genuine probabilities, with no teacher continuation. Synthetic `RELOCATE_START` remains a historical host fixture, not a native game permission.

Among **32 matched DEVELOPMENT fixtures**, switch training versus sham training changed mean ink by **−1.5625 cells** (paired percentile bootstrap 95% interval **[−4.3125, +0.0312]**), with 3 painting wins, 22 ties and 7 losses. The switch policy freshly painted **four fewer instructed target epochs** (71 versus 75), 95% interval for paired per-episode epoch difference [−0.2812, 0]; its two STOP events arose from model-selected host-legal actions. Against the frozen prior, switching painted +2.594 cells/episode but reached three fewer instructed target epochs; neither effect is stable across these two optimizer seeds.

**Seed heterogeneity:** seed 619 produced *identical* full DEVELOPMENT aggregate scores for sham and switch (35 active targets, 143.062 painted cells and zero early STOP per 16 episodes), whereas seed 620 lost four active target epochs and incurred two premature STOP events after switch training. This is an independent negative replication of the earlier RETARGET-DAGGER-001 failure to outperform the frozen prior, although its specific paired switch-versus-sham effect differs from that predecessor. The changed sample budgets and fixtures prohibit pooling both campaigns as one homogeneous causal estimate. Identical aggregate scores do not by themselves prove identical per-action traces or weights.

## Limitations and next falsification

This is synthetic DEVELOPMENT evaluation, not structurally independent HELDOUT, native CyberSand execution, or evidence of reliable 128+ effective gameplay decisions. Goal targets differ in geometry and some active epochs are missed; a full eight-step switch window does not prove convergence. Teacher labels are hand-engineered and may conflict with actual downstream goal attainment. Two seeds are insufficient for a stable generalization claim, and the apparent negative effect on one seed must not be overinterpreted. The experiment did **not** train d1-2B, LFM2.5-350M, Sprocket-500M, or any external model weights.

**Follow-up:** stop extending the current teacher imitation as though it necessarily improves goal navigation. Instead construct TRAIN-only post-switch mistakes with demonstrable goal-regret and train on *actual* corrective actions, then evaluate a frozen preregistered cohort with at least three optimizer seeds and a no-switch/sham control. Keep the sealed structural heldout untouched until the evaluation protocol is frozen; physical CyberSand acceptance remains a separate owner-gated step.
