# QL-020 — Q4 branch calibration robustness on neural score logs and prospective surrogate worlds

**Status:** bounded offline experiment completed; source-specific evidence available; neural on-policy qualification **HOLD**. Scope: `experiments/driv3r/quant_lab/**`, DRIV3R research only. No native CyberSand modifications. No new training and no changes to existing quantized model bits.

## Question

QL-018's seven-byte Q4 branch-logit correction moved Branch Blackout and Branch Mania toward the intended direction on held-out *recorded* states. QL-019's actual W0 rollouts (using surrogate score regressors rather than original neural models) found that the same full-strength correction severely suppressed Mania's branching and reduced coverage. Does attenuating the Q4 correction, or protecting high-novelty/recent-branch contexts, improve robustness without inventing a new game host policy?

## Main measurements

The two score regressors achieve **93.04%** (Blackout) and **95.61%** (Mania) held-out *masked action* agreement against actual QL-017 logged neural scores; their serialized sizes are **1,091,275** and **1,315,759 bytes**, respectively. Do not confuse these large surrogate models with the original quantized worm or its seven-byte Q4 correction.

| Personality | Intervention | Recorded neural BRANCH on held-out states | Prospective 10% mean unique cells | Prospective 10% BRANCH / RETRACE | Prospective 0% mean unique cells |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Blackout | Unmodified | 0/388 | **25.89** | 8 / 62 | **35.44** |
| Blackout | Full Q4 | 31/388 | 24.56 | 45 / 87 | 33.06 |
| Blackout | Half Q4 | 3/388 | **25.89** | 8 / 62 | **35.44** |
| Blackout | Cap ±0.30 | 0/388 | 25.89 | 8 / 62 | 35.44 |
| Blackout | Freshness collar | 1/388 | 25.89 | 8 / 62 | 35.44 |
| Blackout | Constant delta | 32/388 | 26.50 | 49 / 91 | 33.94 |
| Mania | Unmodified | 129/387 | 26.39 | 197 / 54 | **36.61** |
| Mania | Full Q4 | 82/387 | 25.56 | 27 / 68 | 36.22 |
| Mania | Half Q4 | 109/387 | **28.11** | **186 / 80** | 36.33 |
| Mania | Cap ±0.30 | 129/387 | 26.39 | 197 / 54 | **36.61** |
| Mania | Freshness collar | 124/387 | 25.11 | 185 / 50 | 36.17 |
| Mania | Constant delta | 70/387 | 28.06 | 115 / 75 | **36.61** |

The original Mania Q4 correction collapses stochastic branching from 197 to 27 across 18 matched episodes, while half-strength preserves 186 and raises mean painted coverage by **1.72 cells** relative to unmodified Mania. That is a useful, albeit small-cohort, intervention effect. The same half-strength correction produces **no on-policy change for Blackout** in these worlds and therefore fails the symmetric recovery objective. The constant correction nearly matches Mania's coverage advantage, so the result does **not** isolate contextual adaptation as the cause.

Recorded QL-017 shadow tests confirm the effect *without fitting a surrogate*: Mania greedy BRANCH frequency moves from 129/387 to 82/387 under full Q4, but only to 109/387 under half-strength. On Blackout it moves from 0/388 to 31/388 under full Q4 and only 3/388 under half strength. Retaining a fraction of the quantized correction is **not a symmetric universal repair**.

## Runtime and caveats

The two Q4 residuals each occupy **7 serialized bytes**. The proxy itself is approximately 1.1–1.3 MB, and shared-process peak RSS was measured, not per-policy RSS. Reference CPU inference on the shared process for the Mania surrogate was approximately **0.90 ms median per decision unmodified** and **1.03 ms with half-Q4** (eight validation states, warmed once); these are noisy Python/ExtraTrees numbers, not low-bit-kernel timings or language-model tokens/sec. Deterministic W0 actions/sec are not language-model tokens/sec.

This remains an **experimental negative for a one-size-fits-all correction** and a positive for retaining Mania's distinctive branching while limiting overcorrection. Behavioural promotion remains HOLD. The next decisive test is to hydrate the exact QL-003 → QL-014 → QL-015 → QL-017 original inference dependency graph, verify exact historical scores on recorded observations, and only then run new corrected neural on-policy W0 trajectories. Keep the original Blackout and Mania as immutable specimens; no native CyberSand integration is authorized.
